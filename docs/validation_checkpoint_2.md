## 🔍 Detailed Agreement & Divergence Analysis

### 1. Points of Strong Agreement (Where the Agent hit the target)
* **Backbone VRAM Bottleneck Identification:** The agent correctly diagnosed that training a full **CNN-transformer-based BiT** (or Swin-B) backbone on 20,000 image pairs will trigger Out-Of-Memory (OOM) errors on an 8GB VRAM RTX 5050 GPU.
* **Lightweight Backbone Substitution:** The agent independently recommended replacing the heavy feature extraction network with a lightweight alternative (**Swin-T** or **ResNet-18**), which aligns perfectly with your thesis methodology of swapping heavy encoders for lightweight heads.
* **Freeze vs. Train Analysis:** The agent agreed that the primary visual encoders should remain frozen or adapted via small modules (like SFN) to protect local compute budgets.

### 2. Points of Divergence (Strict Critic)
* **Text Encoder Fine-Tuning Suggestion:** 
  * *Agent Output:* Under `Text Encoders`, the agent suggested: *"Do not freeze the CLIP text encoder during training. Instead, use a different text encoder that can be fine-tuned..."*
  * *Strict Critique:* This is a **divergence**. Unfreezing the CLIP text encoder is highly compute-intensive and would worsen VRAM usage on an 8GB GPU. The real thesis notes correctly kept the CLIP text encoder frozen and relied purely on context prompt tokens (CoOp) to align semantics, which is much more compute-efficient.
* **Explicit Head Details:**
  * *Agent Output:* The agent suggested replacing the Swin Transformer Decoder with an abstract "different decoder module that can be fine-tuned."
  * *Strict Critique:* The agent's suggestion is a bit too generic compared to your thesis, which explicitly swapped the Swin decoder for a **lightweight convolutional segmentation head**.
